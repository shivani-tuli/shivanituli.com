Survivin drives renal cell carcinoma tumorigenesis via proliferative and metabolic adaptation

Shivani Tuli, Gabrielle Inserra, Yamato Murakami, Cais Vo, Sarvesh Senguttavan, Yongho Bae, Dave Gau

Disease Models & Mechanisms, Volume 19, Issue 8, August 2026

DOI 10.1242/dmm.052706  |  PubMed Search PubMed  |  Preprint bioRxiv

Publisher Full text at Disease Models & Mechanisms

Abstract

Survivin, encoded by the BIRC5 gene and a member of the inhibitor of apoptosis protein (IAP) family, is frequently overexpressed in renal cell carcinoma (RCC) and is associated with poor patient prognosis. This study investigates the multifaceted oncogenic role of survivin in RCC using both loss-of-function (shRNA-mediated knockdown) and pharmacological inhibition (YM155/sepantronium bromide) approaches in human 786-O and murine RENCA cell lines.

Survivin depletion significantly reduced cell proliferation, impaired S-phase entry, downregulated Cyclin D1 expression, and inhibited both single-cell and collective migration. Beyond its canonical roles in apoptosis inhibition and cell-cycle regulation, survivin was found to be essential for maintaining mitochondrial architecture and bioenergetics. Loss of survivin led to an accumulation of dysfunctional mitochondria — likely due to impaired mitochondrial clearance — and a reduction in cellular oxygen consumption rates.

These findings establish survivin as a critical node that integrates cell-cycle progression, cytoskeletal organization, and mitochondrial homeostasis in renal cell carcinoma. The dual proliferative and metabolic vulnerability created by survivin inhibition identifies it as both a prognostic biomarker and a compelling therapeutic target, with implications for combination strategies pairing survivin inhibition with metabolic or cell-cycle-directed therapies in advanced kidney cancer.

Key Findings

  • Survivin is overexpressed in RCC and correlates with poor prognosis in clear cell renal cell carcinoma (ccRCC) patient cohorts.
  • Survivin depletion reduces proliferation by impairing S-phase entry and downregulating Cyclin D1 expression in RENCA and 786-O cell lines.
  • YM155 pharmacological inhibition phenocopies genetic survivin knockdown, reducing proliferation, migration, and altering mitochondrial morphology.
  • Survivin maintains mitochondrial homeostasis: its loss causes accumulation of dysfunctional mitochondria with impaired clearance and reduced oxygen consumption.
  • Survivin couples proliferation with metabolism, acting as a critical node linking cell-cycle progression, cytoskeletal organization, and bioenergetics.
  • Survivin inhibition impairs migration: both single-cell and collective cell migration are significantly reduced upon survivin depletion.
  • Therapeutic implications: survivin represents a dual vulnerability, suggesting combination strategies with metabolic or cell-cycle-directed therapies for advanced RCC.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the role of survivin (BIRC5) in renal cell carcinoma?

Survivin (BIRC5) drives renal cell carcinoma tumorigenesis by coupling cell-cycle progression with mitochondrial homeostasis. It promotes S-phase entry, maintains Cyclin D1 expression, supports cell migration, and preserves mitochondrial function. When survivin is depleted, RCC cells accumulate dysfunctional mitochondria and show reduced oxygen consumption, demonstrating that survivin integrates proliferative and metabolic adaptation in kidney cancer.

What is the effect of YM155 on renal cell carcinoma cells?

YM155 (sepantronium bromide), a pharmacological survivin inhibitor, significantly reduces RCC cell proliferation, impairs S-phase entry, downregulates Cyclin D1 expression, and inhibits both single-cell and collective migration in RENCA and 786-O cell lines. YM155 treatment also disrupts mitochondrial morphology and bioenergetics, leading to accumulation of dysfunctional mitochondria.

How does survivin affect mitochondrial function in kidney cancer?

Survivin depletion in renal cell carcinoma cells causes changes in mitochondrial morphology and bioenergetics, leading to an accumulation of dysfunctional mitochondria due to impaired clearance. This results in reduced oxygen consumption rates, indicating that survivin is essential for maintaining mitochondrial homeostasis in RCC.

Is survivin a therapeutic target for kidney cancer?

Yes. Tuli et al. (2026) demonstrate that survivin is both a prognostic biomarker and a therapeutic vulnerability in renal cell carcinoma. Its inhibition disrupts multiple oncogenic pathways simultaneously — proliferation, migration, and mitochondrial function — suggesting combination strategies with metabolic or cell-cycle-directed therapies for advanced RCC.

What cell lines were used in the survivin RCC study?

The study used RENCA (murine renal cell carcinoma) and 786-O (human clear cell renal cell carcinoma) cell lines. Both shRNA-mediated knockdown and pharmacological inhibition with YM155 were performed in both cell lines.

What is the relationship between survivin and Cyclin D1 in RCC?

Survivin maintains Cyclin D1 expression in renal cell carcinoma cells. When survivin is depleted, Cyclin D1 is downregulated, leading to reduced S-phase entry and impaired proliferation. This establishes survivin as a critical upstream regulator of cell-cycle progression in RCC through the Cyclin D1 axis.
