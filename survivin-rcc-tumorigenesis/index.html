Survivin drives renal cell carcinoma tumorigenesis via proliferative and metabolic adaptation

Abstract

Renal cell carcinoma (RCC) is a heterogeneous malignancy in which clear cell renal cell carcinoma (ccRCC) represents the most common and aggressive subtype. While survivin (encoded by the BIRC5 gene)—a member of the inhibitor of apoptosis protein (IAP) family and a master regulator of mitotic progression—is frequently overexpressed in RCC and strongly correlates with poor patient survival, its broader oncogenic role in kidney cancer biology has remained poorly defined.

Using both loss-of-function (shRNA- and siRNA-mediated gene knockdown) and pharmacological inhibition (via the small-molecule survivin suppressant YM155 / sepantronium bromide) in murine (RENCA) and human (786-O) RCC cell models, combined with transcriptomic profiling of the TCGA-KIRC patient cohort (identifying 1,484 upregulated and 479 downregulated differentially expressed genes in advanced-stage tumors), this study explores the multifaceted functions of survivin in RCC tumorigenesis.

Survivin depletion substantially reduced cell proliferation, halted cell cycle progression by impairing S-phase entry, and downregulated Cyclin D1 expression. Moreover, loss of survivin significantly inhibited both collective and single-cell migration. Beyond its canonical roles in apoptosis evasion and mitotic spindle check, survivin was discovered to be essential for maintaining mitochondrial architecture and bioenergetic homeostasis. Survivin deficiency induced an increase in cell size and mitochondrial mass (accumulating compromised, enlarged mitochondria) coupled with a ∼30% reduction in basal oxygen consumption rate (OCR), indicative of defective mitochondrial clearance/turnover.

Collectively, these findings demonstrate that survivin serves as a central oncogenic hub linking cell cycle progression (via the Cyclin D1 axis) with cytoskeletal organization and mitochondrial metabolic adaptation in renal cell carcinoma, establishing survivin as both a key prognostic biomarker and an actionable dual-vulnerability therapeutic target.

Key Findings

  • Survivin is overexpressed in RCC and predicts poor prognosis: TCGA-KIRC analysis shows BIRC5 is markedly upregulated in ccRCC tumors vs. normal kidney tissue. Elevated survivin correlates with advanced stage, higher grade, metastasis, and significantly worse overall survival.
  • Survivin depletion blocks proliferation via Cyclin D1: shRNA/siRNA knockdown and YM155 treatment suppress RCC cell proliferation by impairing S-phase entry and downregulating Cyclin D1 expression in RENCA and 786-O cell lines.
  • YM155 pharmacological inhibition phenocopies genetic knockdown: The small-molecule survivin inhibitor YM155 (sepantronium bromide) simultaneously reduces proliferation, migration, and mitochondrial bioenergetics, validating survivin as a druggable target.
  • Survivin maintains mitochondrial homeostasis: Survivin loss causes accumulation of enlarged, dysfunctional mitochondria (increased TOM20-positive area) with ~30% reduced basal OCR (Seahorse XF), indicating impaired mitochondrial clearance.
  • Survivin couples proliferation with metabolism: Survivin acts as a critical oncogenic node linking cell-cycle progression, cytoskeletal organization, and bioenergetics — not simply an anti-apoptotic factor.
  • Survivin inhibition impairs migration: Both single-cell (transwell/Boyden chamber) and collective cell migration (scratch assay) are significantly reduced upon survivin depletion.
  • Therapeutic implications for combination strategies: The dual proliferative and metabolic vulnerability created by survivin inhibition suggests combination strategies pairing survivin-directed therapy with metabolic or cell-cycle agents for advanced RCC.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the role of survivin (BIRC5) in renal cell carcinoma?

Survivin (BIRC5) drives renal cell carcinoma tumorigenesis by coupling cell-cycle progression with mitochondrial homeostasis. It promotes S-phase entry, maintains Cyclin D1 expression, supports cell migration, and preserves mitochondrial function. When survivin is depleted, RCC cells accumulate dysfunctional mitochondria and show ~30% reduced basal oxygen consumption, demonstrating that survivin integrates proliferative and metabolic adaptation in kidney cancer.

What is the effect of YM155 (sepantronium bromide) on renal cell carcinoma cells?

YM155 (sepantronium bromide), a pharmacological survivin inhibitor, significantly reduces RCC cell proliferation, impairs S-phase entry, downregulates Cyclin D1 expression, and inhibits both single-cell and collective migration in RENCA and 786-O cell lines. YM155 treatment also disrupts mitochondrial morphology and bioenergetics, leading to accumulation of dysfunctional mitochondria. It phenocopies genetic survivin knockdown.

How does survivin affect mitochondrial function in kidney cancer?

Survivin depletion in renal cell carcinoma cells causes changes in mitochondrial morphology and bioenergetics: cells accumulate enlarged, compromised mitochondria (increased TOM20-positive area) but show ~30% reduced basal OCR (Seahorse XF Cell Mito Stress Test). Mitophagy assessment revealed no increased clearance, indicating passive accumulation of dysfunctional mitochondria due to impaired turnover. This demonstrates survivin is essential for mitochondrial quality control in RCC.

Is survivin a therapeutic target for kidney cancer?

Yes. Tuli et al. (2026) demonstrate that survivin is both a prognostic biomarker and a therapeutic vulnerability in renal cell carcinoma. Its inhibition disrupts multiple oncogenic pathways simultaneously — proliferation, migration, and mitochondrial function — suggesting combination strategies with metabolic or cell-cycle-directed therapies for advanced RCC.

What cell lines were used in the survivin RCC study?

The study used RENCA (murine renal cell carcinoma) and 786-O (human clear cell renal cell carcinoma) cell lines. Both shRNA-mediated knockdown, siRNA-mediated knockdown, and pharmacological inhibition with YM155 were performed in both cell lines. Additionally, the TCGA-KIRC patient cohort was analyzed for clinical validation of survivin expression patterns.

What is the relationship between survivin and Cyclin D1 in RCC?

Survivin maintains Cyclin D1 expression in renal cell carcinoma cells. When survivin is depleted, Cyclin D1 is downregulated, leading to reduced S-phase entry and impaired proliferation. This establishes survivin as a critical upstream regulator of cell-cycle progression in RCC through the Cyclin D1 axis.

What does the TCGA-KIRC analysis show about survivin in kidney cancer?

Transcriptomic analysis of the TCGA-KIRC patient cohort shows BIRC5 is markedly upregulated in ccRCC vs. normal kidney tissue, with elevated survivin correlating with advanced stage, higher grade, metastasis, and worse overall survival. The analysis identified 1,484 upregulated and 479 downregulated differentially expressed genes, with survivin emerging as a central hub in PPI network analysis.

Does survivin inhibition affect cancer cell migration?

Yes. Survivin depletion significantly reduces both single-cell migration (transwell Boyden-chamber assay) and collective cell sheet migration (scratch/wound-healing assay) in RENCA and 786-O cell lines. This effect was observed with both genetic knockdown and YM155 pharmacological inhibition, demonstrating survivin's role in cytoskeletal organization and cell motility in RCC.

What is the mechanism of survivin-mediated mitochondrial dysfunction in RCC?

Survivin deficiency induces increased cell size and mitochondrial mass (enlarged, compromised mitochondria visualized by TOM20 immunostaining) with ~30% reduced basal OCR (Seahorse XF). Mitophagy assessment showed no increased clearance, indicating passive accumulation of dysfunctional mitochondria due to impaired turnover — a metabolic vulnerability for therapeutic targeting.
